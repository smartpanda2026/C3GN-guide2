C3 Glomerulopathy (C3GN) — Clinical Guide

Diagnosis · Treatment · Dosing Calculator · Follow‑up Graph

Evidence‑based Updated 2026
Diagnostic triad & key findings
Immunofluorescence

Dominant C3 deposition (≥2+ intensity) with absent or minimal immunoglobulin (IgG, IgM, IgA) and C1q (≤ trace). C3 deposition at least two orders of magnitude greater than any other immunoreactant.

Light microscopy

Membranoproliferative pattern (MPGN) or mesangial proliferation; may show double contours, endocapillary hypercellularity. Moderate-to-marked proliferation suggests more severe disease.

Electron microscopy

Subendothelial, subepithelial, or intramembranous dense deposits; often with mesangial interposition. Dense deposit disease (DDD) is a separate C3G subtype.

Exclusion & timing: Rule out infection, autoimmune disease, and monoclonal gammopathy. If all features resolve by 12 weeks, the patient is not considered to have C3 glomerulopathy (e.g., post-infectious GN).

Clinical pearls
  • C3GN vs. DDD: C3GN has subendothelial/mesangial deposits; DDD has intramembranous dense osmiophilic deposits. Both share alternative pathway dysregulation.
  • Serum C3 levels are low in ~70–80% of C3GN, but normal C3 does not exclude the diagnosis.
  • C3NeF positivity supports diagnosis but is not required; it may guide treatment (e.g., rituximab if autoantibody-driven).
  • Paraproteins can be present without overt myeloma and may drive C3GN; always screen in older adults.

Supportive & general measures (all patients)
BP control: Target <130/80 mmHg; ACEi/ARB preferred.
Proteinuria: Aim <1 g/day; ACEi/ARB, consider SGLT2 inhibitors.
Lipid management: Statins if dyslipidemia.
Edema: Diuretics as needed; salt restriction.
Vaccination: Pneumococcal, influenza, COVID-19.
Monitoring: Urine protein, eGFR, C3 every 3–6 months.
Who to treat with immunosuppression
KDIGO 2021 threshold

Proteinuria >1 g/d plus hematuria, or declining kidney function over ≥6 months.

Lancet review threshold

Proteinuria >1.5 g/24h with other abnormalities; consider indefinite therapy for persistent hematuria plus proteinuria >1.5 g/24h.

Moderate-to-severe disease = moderate-to-marked proliferation on biopsy and proteinuria >2 g/d.
Immunosuppressive & targeted therapies
AgentIndication / RationaleNotes
MMF + glucocorticoids First-line for moderate-to-severe C3G (KDIGO 8.2.2.1) Best-supported regimen. Spanish cohort (n=60): 86% remission, 100% renal survival at 5 yr. Larger cohort (n=97): 79% remission, 14% kidney failure. Relapse in 33% after discontinuation; longer MMF duration reduces relapse. Pathogenic complement gene variants → only partial remission.
Iptacopan (Fabhalta) FDA 2025 Oral factor B inhibitor to reduce proteinuria in adults with C3G APPEAR-C3G: 200 mg BID gave ~7-fold higher odds of ≥50% UPCR reduction with stable/improved eGFR at 6 months vs placebo. First positive RCT. Boxed warning: encapsulated bacterial infection; REMS.
Pegcetacoplan (Empaveli) FDA C3 inhibitor for patients ≥12 years with C3G or primary IC-MPGN Subcutaneous infusion per label. Encapsulated bacterial infection risk; vaccination required. REMS.
Eculizumab (off-label) Refractory or rapidly progressive/crescentic disease Retrospective series (n=26): 23% global response, 54% no response. Normalizes C5b-9 but limited effect on underlying AP dysregulation. Boxed warning: meningococcal infection.
Cyclophosphamide Alternative for crescentic disease (with pulse steroids) Oral or IV. Cytopenias, infection, gonadotoxicity, bladder toxicity.
Danicopan Not used Failed phase 2 C3G endpoints.
Evidence caveat: No randomized trials support older immunosuppressive regimens. The 2025 APPEAR-C3G trial of iptacopan is the first positive RCT. Iptacopan reduces proteinuria without demonstrated short-term eGFR benefit; long-term kidney outcomes remain to be established.
Treatment algorithm (simplified)
  1. Optimize supportive care (ACEi/ARB, BP, SGLT2i).
  2. If proteinuria >1 g/day or declining eGFR → consider immunosuppression.
  3. Moderate-to-severe disease → MMF + glucocorticoids first-line.
  4. If MMF+steroids fail → consider eculizumab or clinical trial.
  5. Rapidly progressive/crescentic → pulse IV methylprednisolone → oral prednisone + cyclophosphamide or MMF; eculizumab may be considered.
  6. Consider FDA-approved targeted therapy (iptacopan or pegcetacoplan) for proteinuria reduction.
Infection prophylaxis before complement inhibitors
  • Vaccinate against S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis (A, C, W, Y, B), and H. influenzae type b per ACIP, ≥2 weeks before first dose of iptacopan, pegcetacoplan, or eculizumab.
  • If urgent therapy needed before vaccination is complete, give antibacterial prophylaxis and vaccinate ASAP.
  • Breakthrough infection can occur despite vaccination, especially with eculizumab; monitor closely. Iptacopan and pegcetacoplan are REMS-restricted.

recurrence within 2 years

Recurrence typically occurs within the first 2 years post‑transplant. Risk factors include:

  • Young age at diagnosis
  • Rapid progression to ESRD (<3 years)
  • Positive C3NeF or autoantibodies
  • Genetic complement variants (e.g., factor H)
  • DDD subtype (higher recurrence than C3GN)
Management of recurrence
StrategyDetails
Pre‑transplant evaluationAssess complement profile (C3, C3NeF, genetics); consider combined liver‑kidney transplant in select cases with factor H deficiency. Screen living donors for complement abnormalities; genetic counseling if familial C3G.
Monitoring post‑transplantUrine protein, creatinine, C3 levels every 3 months for first 2 years; biopsy if dysfunction.
Immunosuppression adjustmentNo standard protocol; consider MMF, steroids. Avoid calcineurin inhibitor? (conflicting data).
Targeted therapyEculizumab has been used in recurrent C3GN with variable success. Rituximab if C3NeF positive. Consider FDA-approved iptacopan or pegcetacoplan for proteinuria reduction.
Novel agentsComplement inhibitors (iptacopan, pegcetacoplan) under investigation for recurrence; long-term outcomes pending.
Key considerations
  • Recurrence vs. rejection: Biopsy with IF is essential; C3 dominant staining suggests recurrence.
  • Graft survival: Recurrence reduces graft survival, but overall outcomes are still acceptable compared to dialysis.
  • Paraprotein-driven disease: If monoclonal gammopathy is present, clone-guided treatment may improve renal outcomes; hematology consultation warranted.
